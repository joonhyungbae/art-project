# Cognitive scaffold

The framework's load-bearing philosophical position. The plugin is neither **inert tool** nor **co-author**; it occupies a middle position the practice-based research literature and the extended-mind tradition both make available: the **cognitive scaffold**.

## The extended-mind move

[Clark & Chalmers 1998](https://academic.oup.com/analysis/article-abstract/58/1/7/153111)'s extended-mind hypothesis: cognition can extend into external scaffolds that meet parity and reliability conditions. The canonical example is Otto's notebook (the notebook used to bear semantic memory load becomes part of the cognitive system, not merely a tool the system uses).

[Malafouris 2013](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262019194/how-things-shape-the-mind/)'s *material engagement theory* pushes the position further: cognition is *constituted* in the engagement with things, not located behind the skin. The clay shapes the potter as much as the potter shapes the clay.

The framework treats the AI in this lineage. The plugin is one external scaffold among others (the notebook, the studio wall, the Oblique Strategies deck, the conversation with a peer). It can be part of the cognitive system without being either inert (a tool the artist uses) or authorial (a co-creator who contributes to the work).

## What this means for the plugin's behaviour

The scaffold position is not a free pass. It is an operational position that demands specific architectural disciplines:

- **Provenance tagging** — the scaffold's outputs are tagged with which traditions grounded them, so the artist can audit the cognitive partner.
- **Refusal to rank** — the scaffold offers candidates and contrasts; the artist does the ranking.
- **Mandatory bias disclosure** — the scaffold tells the artist what it cannot see (training-data canon bias in `lineage`).
- **Architectural friction against simulacrum-of-critique misuse** — the scaffold refuses to substitute for the real interlocutors (the friction warning in `rehearsal`).

These disciplines are what make the cognitive-scaffold position *operational* rather than aspirational.

## The Eno/Schmidt analogy

Brian Eno and Peter Schmidt's *Oblique Strategies* deck (1975) has, for fifty years, occupied exactly the scaffold position in studio practice. Musicians and visual artists treat the deck as neither inert (it is consulted at decision points; its constraint shapes the next move) nor authorial (Eno and Schmidt are not credited as co-authors of works that used the deck). The deck is a stable external partner that produces constraint or provocation, never solution.

The framework treats the plugin operationally the same way: produce constraint or provocation; the artist interprets and accepts or rejects.

## The Penny constraint

[Penny 2017](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262035941/making-sense/) argues for an embodied, situated, enactive epistemology of art-and-engineering practice *against* the representational paradigm LLMs instantiate. The critique is that representational systems mis-model cognition wherever they operate — so scoping the LLM to "articulation only" does not by itself answer the critique.

The framework's response is narrower than refutation. The propositional articulation work the plugin scaffolds (grant applications, doctoral expositions) is *itself* representational by genre. Its target population is already operating in the representational register. The framework commits to that register and names the commitment as a contestable architectural choice rather than a neutrality.

Because Penny's critique applies to the LLM substrate itself rather than its scoping, the framework cannot claim to restore non-representational cognition; it mitigates representational-paradigm harms through architectural transparency — provenance tagging, authentic-practice boundaries, simulacrum-of-critique refusal — of a kind Penny's epistemology can recognise as honestly scoped.

This is conceded openly. The scaffold position does not solve the embodied-cognition critique; it operates honestly within it.

## What the scaffold is not

- **Not a tool.** A tool does what the artist tells it; the scaffold has commitments (refusal to rank, gap-acknowledgement) the artist cannot override.
- **Not a co-author.** A co-author contributes to the work; the scaffold never touches the artwork.
- **Not a coach.** A coach evaluates progress; the scaffold refuses evaluation (no auto-convergence; formative-not-decisional rehearsal).
- **Not a therapist.** A therapist asks about the artist; the scaffold asks about the work.
