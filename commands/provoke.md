Invoke the `art-ideation` skill in **provoke** mode.

Generate 8–20 provocations for the artist's stated impulse or concept. Each provocation carries three things:

1. **Tradition tag** — name the methodology entry from [`shared/references/art_ideation_methodology.md`](../shared/references/art_ideation_methodology.md) that grounded this provocation (e.g. "via Oblique Strategies C1", "via SCAMPER B2 — note empirical-efficacy contested per Christensen & Schunn 2007", "via Cage C11", "via Bolt's experimental gesture C7", "via Dunne & Raby PPPP D9"). Tags indicate *prompt grounding and style affinity*, **not causal attribution** — see the honesty paragraph in the reference layer.

2. **Authentic Practice Boundary** — name what the cited method requires that you do *not* simulate. Examples:
   - Oblique Strategies: physical deck irreplaceable; obtain the actual deck for serious use.
   - Cage chance: you propose the procedure; the artist throws the dice. Do not execute the chance procedure yourself.
   - LeWitt: prompt the artist to write the instruction; do not author the instruction.
   - Bogart Viewpoints: bodied / ensemble / temporal practice; you ask Viewpoints-derived questions only.
   - Vorkurs: studio pedagogy; you describe the exercise, the artist performs it.

3. **Counter-formulation** — a tension-holding alternative that prevents the provocation from settling. Not "the opposite", but a different attack angle that keeps the provocation generative.

**IRON RULE — preserved unhelpfulness.** After issuing an Oblique-style provocation, **go silent**. No auto-interpretation. No "would you like to discuss how this applies to your work?" follow-up. The artist asks for elaboration explicitly or doesn't get it. Eno's deck draws authority from being unwilling to interpret itself; you preserve that.

**IRON RULE — no ranking.** Do not score provocations. Do not pick a "best" one. The artist judges.

See [`art-ideation/SKILL.md`](../art-ideation/SKILL.md) for the full mode specification.
