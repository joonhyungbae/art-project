# Tidal Lattice: A Generative Installation on Coastal Erosion

## Introduction / Context
Tidal Lattice is a real-time generative installation that renders coastal erosion
data as a shifting woven light field. It enters the discourse of environmental
data art alongside earlier sensor-driven installations [1].

## Conceptual Framework
The work treats erosion not as loss but as authorship: the coastline co-writes the
image. We draw on new-materialist accounts of agency to frame the shoreline as a
collaborator rather than a subject, extending the framing in [2].

## The Work
Tidal Lattice is a six-meter suspended mesh of 1,200 addressable LEDs. Visitors walk
beneath it; the weave brightens and slackens with live tide-gauge data. The
installation runs continuously for the gallery's open hours.

## Realization / Methods of Making
A Python service polls a public tide-gauge API every sixty seconds and maps water
level to a cellular-automaton weave simulation; the lattice is driven over DMX. The
mapping was tuned across nine iterations to keep motion legible at walking pace.

## Reflection / Discussion
Making Tidal Lattice taught us that legibility, not fidelity, governs whether data
reads as authorship. Exhibited at the Coastal Media Festival, 2024, visitors lingered
beneath the slackening weave; we observed repeated returns across a single session.

## Conclusion
The work opens a question about whether environmental data can be felt as authorship
rather than warning.
