# Creative / Media-Art Terminology Glossary

Replaces the ARS IRB + psychometric glossaries as the **active glossary set** for art-paper. Consumed by the pattern-protection layer and reviewers to keep terminology precise and to catch the genre's characteristic terminology drift.

> Like the ARS glossaries, the purpose is **disambiguation**: pairs of terms that look interchangeable but are not, where conflating them is a substantive error a reviewer will catch.

---

## 1. Practice-based vs. practice-led research

- **Practice-based research** — the creative artifact (the artwork) is the primary contribution; the paper articulates the knowledge embedded in/around it. *No artwork → not practice-based.*
- **Practice-led research** — the contribution is primarily **about practice itself** (insights into the nature of the practice); the artifact may be illustrative rather than the contribution.
- ✗ Drift to avoid: calling a paper "practice-based" when there is no authored artwork, or using the two terms interchangeably.

## 2. Documentation vs. the work

- **The work** — the artwork as it exists/is encountered (installation, performance, software, object, intervention).
- **Documentation** — the *record* of the work (video, stills, code, diagrams). Documentation stands in for the work in the paper but **is not** the work.
- ✗ Drift: treating a render or a video as if it were the work itself when making experiential claims.

## 3. Generative vs. interactive vs. autonomous

- **Generative** — system produces output via process/rules/models, possibly without runtime input.
- **Interactive** — output is shaped by audience/participant input at runtime.
- **Autonomous** — operates without human intervention over time. (Often over-claimed; requires a realization anchor.)
- ✗ Drift: labeling a one-shot generated output "interactive," or a scripted sequence "autonomous."

## 4. Authorship / collaboration credit

- **Sole authorship** — one author conceived and realized the work.
- **Collaboration** — multiple contributors; roles (concept, code, fabrication, sound, performance) should be named.
- **Commissioned / collective / studio** — institutional or collective authorship with specific credit conventions.
- ✗ Drift (integrity flag): describing collaborative work as solo, or omitting named contributors. Mirrors ARS authorship-integrity concern.

## 5. Copyright / rights terms (replaces IRB anonymity cluster)

- **Copyright** — the author's rights in the work; cite others' works and images with permission/credit.
- **Exhibition / display rights** — distinct from copyright; the right to show a work.
- **Image credit / courtesy line** — required attribution for reproduced images ("Courtesy of the artist / Photo: …").
- **Moral rights** — attribution and integrity rights, jurisdiction-dependent.
- ✗ Drift: reproducing others' work or install photos without a courtesy line / permission note.

## 6. Medium vs. material vs. format

- **Medium** — the artistic category (e.g., generative video, interactive installation, net art, bio-art).
- **Material** — the concrete stuff (LED, projection, custom PCB, mycelium, ML model).
- **Format** — delivery/exhibition form (single-channel, multi-screen, web, performance duration).
- ✗ Drift: collapsing these so the reader can't tell what the work physically is.

## 7. Concept vs. theme vs. technique

- **Concept** — the idea the work embodies/investigates (the load-bearing contribution).
- **Theme** — subject matter / topic.
- **Technique** — the how (method of making).
- ✗ Drift: a paper that describes technique and theme but never states the concept — the most common art-paper weakness.

## 8. Reception terms (precision required by the evidence model)

- **Observed response** — what was actually seen/recorded with audiences (anchorable).
- **Critical reception** — reviews/criticism by named writers (citable).
- **Anecdotal** — informal, must be labeled as such.
- ✗ Drift (integrity flag): "audiences were moved/amazed" with no observable anchor → reception inflation.

---

## Usage in the pattern-protection layer

art-paper reviewers and the integrity gate use this glossary the way ARS used its IRB/psychometric glossaries: when an agent emits a term from a contrasting pair, check it is the *correct* member for what is described. The reception (§8), authorship (§4), and autonomy (§3) clusters double as integrity-gate flags per `art_research_evidence_model.md` §4.
